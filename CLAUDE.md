# CLAUDE.md: Manditha Madushanka Portfolio

This file is the design brief and the working rules for this repo. Read it before every task.

## 1. What this site is for

A personal portfolio for **Manditha Madushanka**, who has just completed a BSc (Hons) in Information Technology at the University of Kelaniya (Sri Lanka), and a former Software Engineer Intern in the aeroPORT team at Information Systems Associates (an airline Departure Control System). He is applying for **Associate Software Engineer** roles.

**Primary audience:** recruiters and engineering managers hiring associate SEs. They skim for 30–60 seconds, often on a phone.
**Secondary audience:** people interested in his XR (VR/AR) work, and future freelance XR clients.

**Order of priority, which decides every trade-off:**
1. A recruiter understands within 5 seconds that he is a software engineer with real industry experience (Java, Spring Boot, React, microservices).
2. Fast, accessible, works perfectly on mobile.
3. The XR section stands out as a memorable differentiator. It is not the headline.
4. Visual polish and the 3D moment.

## 2. Content rules (non-negotiable)

- **All facts come from `docs/CONTENT.md`.** Never invent projects, metrics, employers, dates, skills, testimonials or results. If something is missing, leave a visible `TODO:` and tell me. Do not fill the gap.
- Content lives in typed data files under `src/content/` (e.g. `projects.ts`, `experience.ts`, `skills.ts`). Components never hard-code copy.
- Tone: modest, specific, first person, plain English. No hype words ("passionate", "rockstar", "cutting-edge", "world-class"). Prefer concrete nouns and verbs.
- Never publish his phone number or street address. Contact uses email, LinkedIn and GitHub only.
- aeroPORT is proprietary. Describe only what is in `docs/CONTENT.md`. No screenshots or code from it.
- Always credit the ElectricalSafetyVR team and third-party assets.

## 3. Stack

- **Next.js (App Router, latest stable) + TypeScript (strict)**, statically generated wherever possible.
- **Tailwind CSS** (latest), with design tokens as CSS variables.
- **Motion** (the `motion` package, formerly Framer Motion; import from `motion/react`) for UI animation.
- **React Three Fiber + @react-three/drei** for the single 3D element only.
- Video: a YouTube facade (a click-to-load thumbnail, e.g. `react-lite-youtube-embed`), using the `youtube-nocookie` domain.
- Deploy: **Vercel**. Package manager: **pnpm** (unless I say otherwise).
- Ask before adding any dependency that is not listed here.

## 5. Design direction

- Clean, confident, engineering-led. Lots of whitespace, strong type, one accent colour.
- **Inspiration only:** awwwards portfolios and zesiger.net. **Never copy** their layouts, assets, code or copy.
- **Theme:** dark by default, plus a light theme. Respect `prefers-color-scheme`, with a manual toggle stored in `localStorage` (wrapped in try/catch).
- **Tokens (CSS variables on `:root`):** `--bg`, `--surface`, `--text`, `--text-muted`, `--border`, `--accent` (a single electric cyan/teal, which nods to XR), `--accent-contrast`, `--accent-ink` (accent-coloured text, `text-accent-ink`: equal to `--accent` in dark, darker in light so it stays AA on a floor line). Every text/background pair must meet WCAG AA contrast.
- **Light theme ("Mist"):** a blue-grey page (`--bg` #e4ebee fading to #dce5e9 down the document, `--bg-wash`), opaque cards (#f3f6f7, border #c5d1d6) with a soft shadow (`light:shadow-card`), text #0f1b24 / muted #44525d, accent #0b6e66 (ink #095c56, button hover #095e57). Its solid blocks (poster, boot blocks, Build shapes) take three tones by the way a face looks, top #cfe3e0, left #a9c6c2, right #8fb2ae, with #0b5550 edges (`--solid-*`), and the hero's glow becomes a soft teal halo *behind* the cube (z -1 in the root stacking context, under the 3D canvas and the poster; never drawn over a block, since blending with the WebGL layer is not reliable). **The dark theme must never change when light changes:** light-only values live only in the light selectors (`:root[data-theme="light"]` and its no-JS copy) or the `light:` variant, never on the dark `:root`; the 3D material and the poster's block switch to the solid faces only while the light-only `--solid-*` tokens exist.
- **Typography:** one sans for UI and body (e.g. Inter or Geist via `next/font`), and one mono for small labels and tech tags (e.g. Geist Mono / JetBrains Mono). Fluid type with `clamp()`.
- **Layout:** mobile-first; max content width ~1100px; 16px side gutter on mobile; no horizontal scroll at 320px.
- **One surface.** The home page sits on one background, the perspective floor (§5a, SCAN), fixed behind every section from hero to footer, on every device: accent lines converging to a horizon like roads seen from slightly above, at the same strength in every section; cards stay opaque on top. Every line is a soft band of one even weight (never a hard 1px stop, which aliases into brighter and fainter stripes on the tilted plane), so lines only thin and fade with distance; the floor fades out smoothly towards the horizon and, with a soft vignette, towards the left and right edges. It is live on capable desktops and the same lines, still, everywhere else (phones, Motion off, no scroll-driven animations), so the design looks the same. It appears once the browser is idle after load (`data-floor`, set by `ScanObserver`, fading in), so its large composited plane never delays first paint (LCP); in the cube boot (§5a) it rolls out from under the cube instead. The hero has no background of its own (its cube floats over the floor in a soft glow). **The VR / AR Projects section is a different "mode" on the same surface:** the floor brightens there (a view timeline where it runs live, otherwise `data-floor-xr` from an IntersectionObserver), one scan sweep runs along it as the section enters (wherever Motion is on), and its cards keep an accent glow. Case-study headers keep a faint flat grid (they have no floor). Floor strengths are tokens (`--floor-strength`, `--floor-strength-xr`; dark .11 / .18, light .18 / .22) chosen so every text colour on a line pixel stays AA in both themes (light accent text is the limit, hence `--accent-ink`).
- **Motion tokens:** every UI duration and easing comes from the tokens in `globals.css` (`--dur-fast|base|medium|slow|sweep|floor-sweep`, `--delay-sweep`, `--ease-out`, `--ease-assemble`, `--ease-piston`; the `duration-fast|base|medium|slow` classes), mirrored for Motion and WAAPI in `src/lib/motion-tokens.ts`. No raw ms or easing values in components. Exceptions: sequencing constants (the cube boot in `src/lib/boot.ts`, CountUp) and the 3D timings in §6.
- **Motion:** short (150–400ms), purposeful; reveal-on-scroll at most once per element. The one-shot SCAN sweeps are single passes and run longer (a card ~600 ms, the floor ~1.2 s). Scroll-scrubbed motion (The Build, §6) has no duration of its own; only its damping follows this range. The one timed exception is the **cube boot** (~3 s on desktop, ~2 s on phones and tablets, once per visit; §5a). Nothing loops in the UI except the 3D elements (the status caret blinks only while it types). Every effect follows the site motion switch and the Signature language (§5a).

## 5a. Signature language

The site's motion is drawn from Manditha's own projects. New motion must use one of these three motifs, or not exist.

- **ASSEMBLE** (from the hero cube, "systems from parts"): elements snap together from parts. Used by: section labels ("01 / Work") assembling character by character (`AssembleText`), the hero cube's idle pulse (it separates and reassembles every few seconds after ~4 s without input; `parts-cube.tsx`), and **The Build** (§6), one continuous companion: in the cube boot the hero cube grows out of its core at the viewport centre (the cube boot, below); as each home section scrolls in, a thin accent **build beam** rises through a free box beside the heading and prints one small voxel model layer by layer; as the section scrolls away the beam falls through it and dissolves it top down, and the next section's box prints the next: Hero the assembled cube, Work a three-layer stack (database, API, UI), VR/AR a VR headset, Experience an aeroplane (the airline system), Writing an open book, Skills a tile grid whose last tile drops into its slot, About a blocky person with a VR headset pushed up on its forehead and an open laptop held in front, Contact an envelope (`src/lib/build-shapes.ts`). While formed and on screen, each model plays a small live action (§6). The **header mark** (`HeaderMark`, left of the name) is the model's dock: it shows the current model in miniature, so the object is always visible (large at a stop, small in the header). While the 3D companion runs it switches as each stop's beam starts printing and holds the shape between stops (`src/lib/build-dock.ts`); otherwise it follows the active section. It is static SVGs, drawn at build time from the same voxel data (`src/lib/build-marks.ts`, sprite route `/build-marks.svg`, `--mark-*` tokens), no WebGL, so it works on phones too (from 360px wide). A new mark builds up from the bottom in block-like rows behind a 1px accent beam while the old one drains away; with motion off it just swaps.
  The **header progress beam** (`.header-progress`) is the build beam laid flat: a 1px accent line on the header's bottom edge that fills left to right with page scroll (a CSS scroll timeline; hidden where unsupported and with motion off).
  The **cube boot** (the home page's opening, ~3 s on desktop, ~2 s below 64rem via `--boot-k`; timings in `src/lib/boot.ts`, written as `--boot-*` CSS variables; the growth's cells, curves and counter in `src/lib/boot-growth.ts`; CSS in "Cube boot" in `globals.css`) is ASSEMBLE too: the hero cube (3×3×3, its hidden centre included as the core) grows at the viewport centre. 0–0.2 s: a dark page with a faint horizon glow (`BootStage`). 0.2 s: one glowing core block appears. 0.5 s: the 6 face blocks slide out of it along their axes, piston-like (`--ease-piston`: short ease-out, tiny overshoot), each growing from 0.6 size and flashing accent as it snaps in. 0.9 s: the 12 edge blocks slide out of a face block the same way. 1.3 s: the 8 corner blocks lock in out of an edge block, and the core's glow dims. Under the cube a plain mono part counter (server-rendered spans revealed by CSS) reads "assembling 1 / 27" → "7 / 27" → "19 / 27" → "27 / 27 · ready", each as its step lands. 1.6–2.1 s: the cube settles onto the floor, which rolls out from under it (transform and opacity). 2.0–2.95 s: the cube glides into its hero slot (FLIP) while three of its blocks detach (`BootBlock`, the poster's own block, flying from its spot on the cube at that block's size): one into the header mark, which then builds; one onto the status pill's dot, which pops in; one onto "View my work", which expands from it and then fills left to right. Meanwhile the hero content reveals in reading order (the counter travels into the status pill, whose border then draws; the name's words slide up from clip wrappers, then one beam sweeps across it; tagline, intro, buttons, socials), and the header builds (its bottom line draws; the name slides out from behind the mark; the nav links drop in, then CV, Pause and theme), finishing before the hero text. At 3.0 s the status types out, the 3D cube takes over (§6) and the reticle wakes. The whole story is the SVG/CSS poster on every device; on capable desktops a WebGL copy of the growth may stand in for the poster's blocks while it grows (§6). Rules: once per visit (`sessionStorage`); a return visit, back/forward, a deep link (`#hash`), a hidden tab, Motion off, reduced motion and no JS all show the finished page from the first frame, because `data-boot="play"` is only set by an inline script in `<head>` before first paint (`bootInitScript`) and every boot style is scoped to it, with the finished page as the resting style. Any scroll, wheel, press, touch or key (the skip link and header work at any moment), a width change, the tab hiding or a Motion change skips to the finished state (`data-boot="skip"`: no typing); the clock (a no-op animation on `BootStage`) ending sets `data-boot="end"`. FLIP "first" offsets (the cube, the counter, the three blocks) are measured in the first animation frame by an inline script at the end of the hero (`bootMeasureScript`) and written as one small stylesheet, so nothing forces a layout of its own and React's elements stay as rendered; the end of every FLIP is the real layout. Transform, opacity and clip-path only; real text stays in the DOM and the accessibility tree throughout.
- **SCAN** (from AR Elemental Arena): AR tracking corners, a single scan sweep, the ring reticle. Used by: a one-time scan sweep and hover/keyboard-focus tracking corners (`ScanFrame` + `ScanObserver`) on the home page's project, VR/AR and experience cards and on the case studies' challenge cards and gallery tiles; the **perspective floor** (`PerspectiveFloor`, `.floor` in `globals.css`), the home page's one background: a faint accent grid receding to a horizon, whose lines come towards the viewer as the page scrolls (a CSS scroll timeline, one cell per repeated iteration; no JS), brighter while VR/AR crosses it (a view timeline), with one scan sweep along it the first time VR/AR enters (`ScanObserver` sets `data-floor-scan`); live (advancing and view-timeline brightening) on large screens with a fine pointer and Motion on only; everywhere else the same lines are still, behind every section, and VR/AR brightens them through `data-floor-xr`; the hero's **ring reticle lying on the floor** (`HeroReticle`, rendered inside `PerspectiveFloor`, so it sits behind the cube): projected from the pointer's height using the floor's own CSS geometry, so it shrinks and flattens towards the horizon and hides above it; kept quiet: an 18px ring, a ~1px stroke at any depth, 40% opacity, no glow; hero cube's column only: shown only while the pointer is around or below the hero visual (its box plus 48px, down to the hero's bottom, below the header) and clipped to that zone, so it never appears over the hero's text column or in another section, and the cursor stays the normal one; large screens with a fine pointer and Motion on; The Build's beam (a single sweep per print or dissolve); the creature viewer's lock-on, where corners grow in around the summon card on each summon and one sweep runs the first time (`.lock-corners` / `.lock-line`, separate from the card's own `.scan-*` so the card's hover and sweep never reach them).
- **PIPELINE** (from PPCH, Sense → Analyze → Act): small steps that check, then resolve. Used by: count-up measured numbers (`CountUp`, values listed per item in `countUp` in the content files, including the project cards' stat chips) and The Build's stack, where a request block steps UI → API → database and back, pausing at each layer. Not used in the hero.

Global rules for every effect:
- No new dependencies (Motion, CSS and small client islands only).
- Off when site motion is off (one exception: SCAN tracking corners are a hover/focus state, not motion, so they still appear with motion off, static, with no lock-on). `data-motion="on" | "off"` on `<html>` is the single switch (`src/lib/motion-pref.ts`, hook in `src/lib/use-motion-on.ts`). It is set before first paint from the stored choice, else `prefers-reduced-motion`. An explicit click on the Motion toggle wins over the OS default and is saved in `localStorage` (try/catch). CSS uses the `motion-on:` variant, never `motion-safe:`.
- Nothing delays or hides the hero h1 or intro (LCP), except the cube boot (§5a ASSEMBLE): it reveals them by transform, clip and opacity within its ~3 s, once per visit, skippable by any input, and it must keep Lighthouse mobile Performance ≥ 90 (median of 5; shorten its timings, starting with the hero text, if it doesn't).
- Real text is always in the DOM for screen readers and SEO; animated copies are `aria-hidden` overlays that never change layout.
- One-shot effects play once per page load; the cube boot once per visit. (The Build's live actions are 3D loops, not one-shots: §6.)
- Pause when off-screen or the tab is hidden.
- Desktop-only effects also need `(hover: hover)` and `(pointer: fine)`.
- Scroll-linked CSS effects use scroll and view timelines only: no JS scroll listeners. Each needs a fallback for browsers without them (`@supports`).
- The Motion toggle has a fixed accessible name ("Pause animations"), and `aria-pressed` carries the state.

## 6. 3D elements

3D is allowed only where it shows my real work. Every piece follows these rules:

- **Lazy and gated.** Load it with `next/dynamic` (`ssr: false`), and only when the gate passes: large screen with a fine hover pointer (`CAPABLE_QUERY`), site motion on, WebGL2 available and no Save-Data (helpers in `src/components/three/gate.tsx`). Phones, Motion off (`data-motion="off"`, which defaults from `prefers-reduced-motion`) and no WebGL never download the chunk or any model. One exception to "when the browser is idle": the cube boot's desktop 3D growth (below), requested as the page's JS first runs, and only while the boot plays.
- **Poster first.** A server-rendered poster (SVG or a screenshot) renders first. The hero's poster is the solid voxel cube, drawn flat at the 3D blocks' exact size and angle, block by block (`HeroPoster`: 27 `<use>`s of one `cube-block` `<symbol>` in the `/build-marks.svg` sprite, painted far to near, which keeps the document small; the symbol holds the dark tones and, shown only in light, the solid faces by side, `blockSidePaths`). The Build's material (`build-material.ts`) has the same split: its dark branch is unchanged, and a light branch (`uSolid`) tones faces top/left/right by the poster's rule, so the two match at rest; both re-read the tokens live when the theme toggles (`onThemeChange`). It is the LCP-safe loading state and the fallback for any failure (chunk, WebGL, context loss, model fetch). The canvas takes the poster's exact place and size, so there is no layout shift.
- **Cheap to run.** DPR capped at 1.5, `frameloop="demand"` (render only while something moves), paused when off-screen or the tab is hidden, and geometries, materials and textures disposed on unmount.
- **Small assets.** Procedural where possible. Any model must be < 500 KB, load only when needed and be cached after. Use `GLTFLoader` from `three/examples/jsm`; no drei and no new dependencies.
- **Decorative canvas.** `aria-hidden`, never traps focus or scroll, and the page is fully usable without it. Any interaction also has a keyboard path through real buttons.

The pieces:

1. **The Build: the hero cube as one continuous scroll companion** (`parts-cube.tsx`, shapes `src/lib/build-shapes.ts`, poses `build-poses.ts`, live actions `build-live.ts`, material `build-material.ts`, scroll mapping `build-scroll.ts`, gate `hero-visual.tsx`). Procedural, one instanced mesh of 100 blocks, loaded when the browser is idle after first paint (during the cube boot the chunk is only fetched; it mounts once the boot has ended or been skipped). It is always the same set of blocks: at each section stop the build beam (one thin plane, `build-material.ts`) rises through its box and prints that section's shape layer by layer, then falls through it and dissolves it as the section leaves. Between stops nothing shows; the header mark holds the shape.
   - **Cube boot (§5a ASSEMBLE):** the whole opening is the SVG/CSS poster on every device, and the poster is drawn at the 3D cube's exact pose. **Desktop exception:** on capable desktops (`CAPABLE_QUERY`, Motion on, no Save-Data, WebGL2; the probe runs once, only there and only while the boot plays) `BootGrowthGate` requests a small 3D growth chunk (`boot-growth.tsx`) when the page's JS first runs, before hydration. Its canvas sits exactly over the poster's cube at the viewport centre and poses the same 27 blocks each frame from the boot's own CSS clock, with the same timings and curves (`boot-growth.ts`). If its first frame comes before the corners start (1.3 s), it goes live (`data-boot-3d="live"`: it fades in as the poster's blocks fade out, at the same pose, so there is no jump); otherwise it never shows. Once the cube has settled (1.8 s, still and in the poster's exact pose) it hands back to the poster (`"done"`), which then glides, and unmounts, so its WebGL context is gone before the companion's. A skip, a failure or a lost context leaves the poster showing, so the hero is never empty. Phones, tablets, no WebGL, Save-Data and Motion off never request it. The h1 and intro are revealed by CSS on the boot's clock, so 3D loading can't delay them. The companion still mounts after the boot, so its hero cube always crossfades in over a finished poster in place; its spin starts from the poster's angle and eases up. Never replayed (Motion toggle, client navigation).
   - **Shapes are data:** each is a low-resolution set of voxel coordinates in `build-shapes.ts` (text-row art or boxes), with an optional view angle, pieces turned about a pivot (the book's pages, the envelope's flap), a placed group that settles in as the shape completes (Skills), an optional per-group block size (the book's thin sheet, the headset's lenses) and an optional live role per group. Blocks a shape doesn't use tuck into its centre at scale 0. The hero cube must keep the poster's exact size and angle (`step: 0.667`). Every shape stays ≤ 100 voxels (`BUILD_BLOCKS`).
   - **Free boxes:** each home section (`main section[id]`, mapped in `SHAPE_BY_SECTION`) gets a box in the hero visual's column, right of its heading and intro and above its cards. The box starts below the previous section's content and ends above the section's `data-build-end` element (or the end of its content). Its left edge clears every `data-build-clear` text in that band. The shape is centred in the box and scaled to fit, with room for its live actions and sway; when there isn't room (fit < 0.36 of the hero cube), the section gets no stop. The hero's box is the hero visual plus 24px, so at scroll 0 the cube sits exactly on the poster, whose own cube then fades; its glows (the halo and the floor glow, `.hero-glow`) then follow the 3D hero cube through `--hero-presence` on the hero visual: they fade as the beam dissolves it and return as it prints back on scroll-up, so the hero never shows an empty glow.
   - **Clipped, page-anchored canvas:** one canvas, absolutely positioned in the document and behind all content (`-z-1`), so it scrolls with the page natively. Each drawn frame, its clipped box (`overflow: hidden`, `data-build-box`) is moved to the blocks' bounds and cut to the current stop's band (from its free box's left edge to the viewport's right edge, between the box's top and bottom). The box also covers the beam, which spans the free box (inside its padding). Never past `clientWidth`, so no horizontal scroll. Every point on the path is safe, so a frame that lags the scroll still can't draw over text or a card. **New content beside a heading needs `data-build-clear`; a section's first card block needs `data-build-end`.**
   - **Scroll mapping (scrubbed, reversible, unhurried):** positions are where a stop's centre sits in the viewport. From 98% to 55% the beam rises through the shape and prints it (each block grows in over ~1 block of beam travel, settling slightly and glowing where the beam touches it). The shape holds from 55% to 15%. From 15% to −2% the beam falls through it and dissolves it top down. The beam's opacity follows its position, not the scroll (`beamOpacity` in `build-poses.ts`): it fades in just before it meets the first block and is gone by the time the last block is complete, so a formed shape never has a line over it, wherever the scroll parks. The header mark switches 15% into a stop's print (`dockAt`). Between stops nothing shows. When sections sit close, a shape first leaves earlier (holding at least until 30%; the hero may leave as soon as you scroll), then the next forms later (formed by 35%), each keeping its full length; only if that isn't enough do the two share the scroll in proportion. The last shape (Contact) finishes forming by the bottom of the page and stays. Progress follows the scroll with ~200 ms damping (`build-scroll.ts`, `build-poses.ts`).
   - **Live actions** (`build-live.ts`; while a shape is formed and on screen, easing in over ~0.6 s and out as it leaves): stack, a request block steps UI → API → database and back; headset, a look-around sway while its lenses pulse softly (per-instance glow); aeroplane, a gentle bank and bob; book, a thin sheet turns over every ~4.5 s, then quietly rises back out of the right-hand pages; tiles, every ~3 s one inner tile lifts and settles back; person, waves every ~6 s with a slight breathing bob while its headset lenses pulse and its laptop screen stays lit; envelope, the flap lifts and a small letter peeks out over the top, then both settle. Every shape also sways slowly and slightly so it reads as 3D.
   - Rendering: every frame while scroll moves the companion along its path (rAF-throttled), on resize, the pointer over the hero, and while a move or ease is in progress; ~30 fps for the hero cube's turn and breathing and for the live actions, and only while the companion is on screen. Nothing otherwise. Stops while the tab is hidden; off with Motion off (unmounted).
2. **AR creature viewer** in the AR Elemental Arena card (`creature-viewer.tsx`, gate `creature-stage.tsx`, models `public/models/ar/*.glb`, Quaternius). It loads the first time the card scrolls into view, not on page load. The four creatures sit over their summon cards: drag to rotate, click or "Animate" to play the next clip, and Fire/Water/Earth/Air chips (each model loads when first chosen). Its controls sit inside the phone frame, so nothing shifts.

The structure must stay ready for a future `/xr` showcase (§4).

## 7. Quality bars (definition of done for every task)

- Lighthouse (mobile): **Performance ≥ 90, Accessibility ≥ 95, Best Practices ≥ 95, SEO ≥ 95.**
- LCP < 2.5s, CLS < 0.1, INP < 200ms. Keep the initial JS small; the 3D bundle is lazy-loaded.
- Accessibility: semantic landmarks, one `h1`, a logical heading order, a skip link, visible focus styles, alt text on every meaningful image, keyboard-reachable everything, `aria-label` on icon-only links.
- Images via `next/image` (AVIF/WebP, explicit sizes). Fonts via `next/font`.
- `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck` (`tsc --noEmit`) and `pnpm build` all pass with zero errors.
- Check the result at widths of 360px, 768px and 1280px.

## 8. Code conventions

- `src/app` (routes), `src/components/sections` (one file per home section), `src/components/ui` (buttons, cards, tags), `src/components/three` (3D only), `src/content` (typed data), `src/lib` (utils).
- Server Components by default; `"use client"` only where interaction or animation needs it.
- Small, readable components; no `any`; named exports for components.
- No inline magic colours. Use tokens or Tailwind theme values.
